Couple Photo Poses That Don't Feel Awkward: 30 Things to Say to Her

Photos can feel awkward when she's being watched and doesn't know what to do with her eyes, hands, or body. “Strike a pose” and “Smile” ask too much. Give her one small thing she can do right away, like “Look to the side, turn back when I say.” It gives her something to focus on, and her face and hands can relax. Here are things you can say to her, grouped into five categories.

1. Start here: Give her something to do, not a pose

Standing in front of the camera feels most awkward when she doesn't know what to do. Saying “Just act natural” doesn't help.

Three things work better:

  • Say one thing at a time. Make it a small action she can do.
  • Give her something to do, like looking away, thinking of something, or taking a few steps. When her attention leaves the camera, her expression relaxes.
  • Wait until she does it, then press the shutter. The moment just after the action often looks most natural.

You can say any of the lines in the five categories below. You don't need to memorize them all. Pick two or three that sound like you.

2. Eyes: Let her look away first

Staring at the camera can make anyone stiff. Have her look somewhere else, then ask her to look back. Her eyes will look more natural as she turns.

What to sayWhen to say it
Look to the side, turn back when I sayShe's facing you and her expression looks stiff
Look at the ground, count to three, then look at meShe keeps staring at the camera and can't smile
Look somewhere far away, not at meYou want a photo where she isn't looking at the camera
Look just past meFor a close-up or waist-up shot without a direct stare
Look toward the lightAnytime you want more light on her face
Okay, now slowly look back at meShe's looking away and you're ready to press the shutter

3. Expression: Give her something to think about

It's hard to smile naturally on command. Give her something to think about or talk about, and a smile can follow. If she's already smiling, you can tell her she doesn't need to.

  • “Tell me the funniest thing from today”
  • “Pretend I just told a terrible joke”
  • “Remember the last time you couldn’t stop laughing”
  • “Relax your mouth and breathe out softly”
  • “Close your eyes, then open them looking at me”
  • “No need to smile, just look at me like you always do”

Press the shutter as soon as she starts to smile. You don't have to wait for her biggest smile.

4. What to do with her hands

Not knowing where to put her hands is a common source of awkwardness. Give them somewhere to go, and keep the movement light.

That last line matters too. Keeping her hands by her face can look more posed. Sometimes she just needs to let them drop.

5. Body and face: Relax and turn a little

Standing straight with her face square to the camera can look like an ID photo. Turning her body or face a little, or finding something to lean on, can help her relax.

  • “Relax your shoulders, let them drop”
  • “Lean on something and relax”
  • “Turn away, then look back at me”
  • “Turn your face slightly, you don’t have to face me”
  • “Tilt your head just a little”
  • “Tuck your chin a little”

For full-body shots, try “Put one foot forward” or “Shift your weight onto one leg.” I cover those in How to Make Legs Look Longer in Full-Body Photos.

6. Take photos while she moves

If she's uncomfortable in front of the camera, ask her to move. When she's walking or turning, she has less time to think about posing, and the photos can feel more like everyday moments.

  • “Walk slowly toward me”
  • “Take a step back, then walk back in”
  • “Spin around once, then stop and look at me”
  • “Walk and glance at the shops”
  • “Fix your clothes, no need to look at me”
  • “Flip your hair, I’ll catch it”

You'll get more misses when she's moving. Take a few photos and pick the best one.

7. How to use Boyfriend Cam's Cheat sheet

All the lines above come from the Cheat sheet in Boyfriend Cam. It has six categories: “Eyes,” “Expression,” “Head & face,” “Hands,” “Body,” and “Move.” It shows one line at a time while you're framing:

  • When the composition lines up and “Now” appears, the Cheat sheet fades in. It doesn't compete with the composition hint. If no composition applies, it can also appear after she's been in the frame for a while.
  • It picks lines based on what she's doing. For example, “No need to smile, just look at me like you always do” appears when she's already smiling. “Okay, now slowly look back at me” appears when she's looking away.
  • For some lines, it can tell whether she followed the prompt, such as looking away and back, smiling, or turning. When she does, the card changes to “She did it — shoot.”
  • Tap for another line. It tries not to repeat lines from the same category back to back. Press and hold to pause that category.
  • If you don't need it, turn off “Cheat sheet” at the top of the screen.
  • When you switch to the front camera for a selfie, the lines change to ones you can say to yourself, such as “Look aside, then slowly back to the lens.”

It never presses the shutter for you. The lines are suggestions. Analysis stays on your phone. Nothing is uploaded, and you don't need an account. Boyfriend Cam requires iOS 17 or later. It's free to download, and you can take 3 photos a day.

8. Common questions

She says, “I don't know how to pose.” What should I say first?

Start with her eyes. Try “Look to the side, turn back when I say.” She only has to turn her head and look back. She doesn't need to pose.

Can I give her several directions at once?

Stick to one at a time. Press the shutter when she does it, then give her the next one. Too many directions can make her more nervous.

What if her smile looks stiff?

Don't ask her to smile. Try “Relax your mouth and breathe out softly” or “Tell me the funniest thing from today” to give her something else to think about.

What if the Cheat sheet line doesn't fit the moment?

Tap the Cheat sheet for another line. Press and hold to pause that category. You can also turn off “Cheat sheet” at the top of the screen.

Will Boyfriend Cam take the photo for me?

No. It gives you hints while you frame the shot. You decide when to press the shutter.
