How to Make Legs Look Longer in Full-Body iPhone Photos

Legs often look short in full-body photos for 2 reasons: you shoot from above while standing, or you leave a lot of ground below her feet. Crouch down and frame her feet near the bottom edge first. That alone helps most photos. Then ask her to change her stance. Here are 5 things you can do with any phone camera.

1. Frame her feet near the bottom edge

When taking a full-body photo, it's easy to put her in the center and leave a lot of ground below her feet. The more empty space there is at the bottom, the smaller she looks and the shorter her legs look.

Adjust the frame so her feet sit near the bottom edge, with just a little space below them. Her legs will take up more of the photo, making her look taller.

2. Crouch down and lower your phone

If you stand and hold your phone at eye level, you're shooting down at her. That can make her legs look shorter. Crouch so your phone is around her waist height or lower. The camera will look slightly upward, making her legs look longer.

You don't need to get down on the ground. Crouch a little, check the frame, and adjust from there. Keep your phone straight as you lower it.

3. Don’t cut at the knees or ankles, and leave a little room above her head

If the bottom edge cuts across her knees or ankles, her legs can look cut off and shorter. For a full-body photo, include her whole feet. For a half-body photo, crop at her waist or chest instead of at a joint.

Leave a little room above her head, too. Don't press it against the top edge, but don't leave so much space that she gets pushed down in the frame. I also cover both points in my previous article on 5 common framing problems.

4. Ask her to change her stance

Standing straight toward the camera with her feet together can look stiff and make her look shorter. Try saying one of these:

  • “Put one foot forward”
  • “Stand sideways, weight on your back foot”
  • “Bend your knees slightly, don’t stand too straight”
  • “Shift your weight onto one leg”

Say just one thing at a time. Wait until she does it, then take the photo.

5. Crop afterward, but know its limits

If you left too much space below her feet, you can crop the photo afterward. Trim the extra space so her feet sit near the bottom edge and her legs look longer. For a story post, a vertical 9:16 crop also works well for a full-body photo.

Cropping changes what's in the frame, not the camera angle. If you shot from above, her legs can still look short after you crop. You need to crouch down while taking the photo, as in tip 2.

6. How Boyfriend Cam guides you

Boyfriend Cam gives you one prompt at a time while you frame the shot:

  • If her feet are too far from the bottom edge, it might say “Crouch a little” or “Phone a little lower” A target ring appears on screen. When the white dot lines up with it, the prompt changes to “Now”
  • If the bottom edge cuts across a joint, it first says “Don’t cut at the knees” or “Don’t cut at the ankles” If there's too little room above her head, it says “Leave room above her head” These prompts take priority over composition hints.
  • If you don't know what to say to her, the “Body” section of the posing guide has lines like “Stand sideways, weight on your back foot” You can say them as written.
  • After you take a photo, a small card shows which composition you used and why. For example, “Full body, longer legs” explains that feet near the bottom edge and a lower camera angle make legs look longer.
  • You can try other suitable crops on a photo you've taken. Choose the original aspect ratio, 3:4, 4:5, 1:1, or 9:16. The app only crops and saves a new photo, keeping the original.

Boyfriend Cam never presses the shutter for you. Its prompts are suggestions; you decide when to take the photo. Analysis stays on your phone. Nothing is uploaded, and you don't need an account. Boyfriend Cam has not been released yet. There is no price to share.

7. Common questions

How low should I hold my phone?

Start by crouching until your phone is around her waist height. If you go too low, you might get too much sky or make her chin look bigger. Adjust a little at a time from waist height.

Do I need to use a wide-angle or 0.5x lens?

No. With a regular 1x lens, crouching down and framing her feet near the bottom edge can still make her legs look longer.

I left too much space below her feet. Can I fix it afterward?

You can crop away the extra space so her feet sit near the bottom edge. But cropping can't fix a downward camera angle. Next time, crouch before taking the photo.

Will Boyfriend Cam take the photo for me?

No. It only gives you prompts while you frame the shot. You decide when to press the shutter.
