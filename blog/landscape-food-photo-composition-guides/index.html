How to Compose Landscape and Food Photos on iPhone: 18 Guide Lines

Landscape and food photos can look messy even when the light is good. The horizon tilts, a path leads nowhere, or the phone is not level over the table. Start by holding your phone straight. Then look for a cue in the scene, choose one guide, and line it up. Here are 18 guides: 10 for landscapes and 8 for food and small objects. Each one tells you when to use it and what to line up.

I’m Sona Orbit, the solo developer of Boyfriend Cam. I added Landscape and Object modes in Boyfriend Cam 1.3 after someone who had bought the lifetime version wrote to ask for composition help with landscapes and objects, too. These 18 guides came with that update. The “When to use it” and “What to line up” columns, along with the quoted tips in this article, use the app’s own wording (as of November 2026).

1. The short version: straighten the phone, then choose a guide

Whether you’re shooting a landscape or something on a table, follow the same order:

  • Start by holding the phone straight. For a landscape, check Level. For a top-down table shot, see whether the two crosses overlap.
  • Look for a cue in the scene: a clear horizon, a path running into the distance, a symmetric building, or exactly three things on the table. That cue tells you which guide to use.
  • Line up one guide at a time. When it lines up, press the shutter. You do not need to try every guide.

When nothing else in the scene gives a cue, use Grid and put the eye-catcher on one of the four intersections.

2. Landscapes: level the phone, then place the horizon

A tilted horizon is easy to spot in a landscape photo. Straighten the phone first, then decide which line should hold the horizon.

GuideWhen to use itWhat to line up
LevelAlways on in Landscape modeWhen the middle joins the two ends in one line, you’re level
GridWhen nothing else in the scene gives a cuePut the eye-catcher on one of the four intersections
Horizon on a thirdWhen there’s a clear horizon over land or seaGreat sky: use the lower line. Great land: use the upper one
HalvesWhen the water mirrors the scene above itPut the waterline on the middle line

With Horizon on a third, either the sky or the land takes the lead. Only a scene like a reflection on water, where the top and bottom match, suits a waterline right in the middle.

3. Landscapes: use lines, foreground, and open space

If the scene has a road, railing, or riverbank, use it to lead the eye into the distance. If it has no clear line, nearby things or open space can give the photo depth.

GuideWhen to use itWhat to line up
Leading linesWhen roads, rails or paths meet in the distanceVanishing point on an intersection; a symmetric corridor in the middle
DiagonalWhen a riverbank, ridge or boardwalk cuts across at an angleBring the line in from a corner, along the dashed line
SymmetryWhen a building, bridge or corridor is symmetricMove sideways until the scene’s axis sits on the center line
ForegroundWhen flowers, grass or rocks are close by at the bottom or a cornerCrouch or stand taller so the near things fill the bottom band
FrameWhen a door, window or arch can frame the viewFit the whole frame in, centered
SpaceWhen open sky, snow or sea holds one small focal pointPut the small subject near a corner, leave the rest empty

Foreground and Space work in opposite ways. Foreground fills the bottom of the photo with nearby things to show depth. Space leaves room around one small subject to draw attention to it.

4. Food and small objects: choose an angle first

When shooting things on a table, choose your angle first. Top-down makes it easier to arrange several things. Eye level helps tall things like bottles and vases look straight.

GuideWhen to use itWhat to line up
AngleAlways on in Object modeThe white dot is the phone’s angle now; the circled one is the suggestion
Top-down tableAppears when the phone points down at a tableWhen the two crosses overlap, the phone is parallel to the table
Level table edgeWhen shooting at 45° or eye level with the table edge in viewStraighten the phone so the edge runs parallel to the dashed line
VerticalsWhen shooting tall, slim things like bottles or vasesHold the phone upright, no tilt, with the bottle’s axis on the center line

Angle has three positions: Top-down, 45°, and Eye level. At 45°, it is easy to tilt the table edge. Keep that edge level so the objects look steady.

5. Food and small objects: arrange the scene

Once you have an angle, decide where each thing goes. Unlike a landscape, you can move the things on a table instead of moving yourself.

GuideWhen to use itWhat to line up
CenteredWhen there’s one thing with a symmetric shape (a plate, a cup rim, a cake)Put its center inside the middle ring
ThirdsWhen one side of it is clear and unclutteredPut it on an intersection, leave the clean side empty
DiagonalWhen shooting two or three things from straight aboveMove things until every dot sits on the diagonal
TriangleWhen there are exactly three thingsSpread the three into a triangle

If you want room for text beside the subject, use Thirds and leave the clear side empty.

6. “Try this”: small moves a guide cannot show

Guides handle position and angle. They cannot show everything, such as changing zoom, moving an object, or waiting for the right moment. In Landscape and Object modes, Boyfriend Cam can show a “Try this” tip when it fits the scene. There are 16 tips, including:

  • “Switch to 0.5× and get close”: when using Foreground and the phone has a 0.5× lens.
  • “Crouch down, closer to the water”: when shooting a reflection.
  • “Move the clutter out of the way”: when the area around the subject is busy.
  • “Add one more small thing to make three”: when there are two things on the table.

You can use these ideas without the app, too. Get close with a wider lens for a foreground shot, crouch for a reflection, and clear the table before taking a photo.

7. Filters: Vivid for landscapes, Tasty for food

Once the composition lines up, choose a look. Boyfriend Cam has 12 filters: Clear, Cream, Airy, Sunlit, Film, Cool, Retro, Cinema, Mono, Soft Mono, plus Vivid for landscapes and Tasty for food. The first option is Original.

  • You can see a filter while framing and adjust its strength.
  • You can also change the filter on the card after shooting or on the screen for changing composition.
  • Skin tones are kept as natural as possible.

8. How to use these in Boyfriend Cam

I added these 18 guides in Boyfriend Cam 1.3, which is on the App Store.

  • Tap the mode button to the right of the shutter to cycle through Portrait, Object, and Landscape. In Settings, “Mode at launch” lets you choose which one opens first.
  • The app looks at the scene and picks a guide. You can tap a different one.
  • When it lines up, the guide changes color and shows “Now.”
  • All analysis happens on your phone, so it works without internet.
  • The free version still lets you take 3 photos a day and use both modes and the filters. Pro removes the photo limit.

9. FAQ

How do I keep the horizon straight in iPhone landscape photos?

Check Level first: When the middle joins the two ends in one line, you’re level. Level is always on in Boyfriend Cam’s Landscape mode.

Should I shoot food from above or at an angle?

Shoot from straight above (Top-down) to arrange several things on a table. Use Eye level for tall things like bottles and vases. Angle in Boyfriend Cam’s Object mode shows whether you’re at Top-down, 45°, or Eye level and circles a suggested angle.

Which guide should I use when there is no clear cue in the scene?

Use Grid. Put the eye-catcher on one of the four intersections.

Can I use Landscape and Object modes in the free version?

Yes. The free version still lets you take 3 photos a day and use both modes and the filters. Pro removes the photo limit.

Do I need internet?

No. All analysis happens on your phone, so it works without internet.
